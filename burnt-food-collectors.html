OSRS Burnt Food & Collector Items

Burnt food can't be sold on the Grand Exchange — but a real, active player-to-player collector market trades it anyway. Here's what actually drives its value, and where prices genuinely get discovered, without us making up numbers we can't stand behind.

Every "burnt food price list" you'll find online — including ones written by an AI — is a guess unless it's citing an actual recent trade. Non-GE items have no market-clearing price; they're worth whatever a specific collector negotiates with a specific seller, in the moment. We'd rather point you at where real prices get discussed than publish a table of numbers we can't verify.

Why burnt food isn't on the Grand Exchange

Cooking in OSRS can fail: undercook a food past its risk window and it burns, becoming a separate, useless item — worth 0 coins from a general store, and never listed on the GE. There are around 48 unique burnt food varieties (a few more counting oddities like ruined herb tea), and every one of them is tradeable directly between players even though none of them are tradeable on the Exchange. That gap — a real item, genuinely tradeable, with zero official market — is exactly what created the collector scene around it.

What actually makes one burnt item rarer than another

Every cookable food has a Cooking level past which it stops burning entirely — a level-99 account cooking sharks essentially never burns one anymore. That single mechanic is the whole rarity engine: foods low-level players burn constantly while training (shrimp, trout, the early fish) are effectively unlimited supply. Foods with a high burn-stop level, or ones tucked behind an annoying multi-step recipe (pies, cakes, anything requiring a specific quest or minigame ingredient first), only get produced when someone deliberately cooks below their usual level — or gets unlucky — just to make one. Scarcity here isn't about drop rate; it's about how much a player has to deliberately go out of their way to generate the item at all.

The bulk-convenience effect

One real, well-documented market dynamic worth knowing: collectors consistently pay a premium for a single large trade over many small ones. Selling a handful of a common burnt item to a collector often gets a lower per-unit offer than selling a genuine bulk stack in one trade — buying in bulk from one seller saves a collector from spending hours doing dozens of tiny trades to reach the same total. If you're sitting on a large stack of anything burnt from a cooking grind, that's worth knowing before you split it up.

Where to actually check current prices

We're not going to publish a price table here — not because the numbers don't exist, but because we can't verify them well enough to put our name on them, and they shift with collector demand anyway. These are the places where real current asking prices actually get discussed:

  • r/2007scape — price-check and collection-showcase posts turn up regularly; search the sub for the specific item.
  • Dedicated collector Discord servers — "RS Burnt" is the largest, with WTB/WTS channels specifically for burnt food, crushed gems, and other non-GE collectibles, and enough active members that asking prices reflect something close to a real market.
  • Trading sites like Sythe — listings tagged for burnt food give a live read on what sellers are currently asking, if not always what buyers actually pay.

If you're the one selling, the same rule of thumb applies everywhere: post a clear count and item name, expect offers to vary a lot between buyers, and remember bulk stacks are worth more per unit than the same items sold off one at a time.

FAQ

Can you sell burnt food on the OSRS Grand Exchange?

No. Selling it to a general store gives 0 coins, and it isn't a GE item at all — it can only be traded directly, player to player.

Why is some burnt food worth more than others?

Every food has a Cooking level past which it stops burning. Low-level foods get burnt constantly and stay common; high-level foods a strong account would rarely burn by accident are scarce, since producing one usually takes a deliberate, low-level (or unlucky) cook.

Where do people actually check current burnt food prices?

There's no live feed — try r/2007scape, dedicated collector Discord servers, or trading sites like Sythe, where current asking prices actually get discussed.
